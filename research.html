From prediction to decision support

The research is designed as a connected system rather than a collection of isolated maps. Observed contaminant values and multi-source environmental predictors are integrated in a reproducible geospatial workflow. Models are evaluated through independent validation, spatial robustness and explainability before predictions are connected to population exposure and monitoring adequacy.

This framing keeps uncertainty visible and treats governance as a research output: not simply where contamination is predicted, but where the combination of severity, exposure, settlement transition and insufficient monitoring should inform attention.

Four connected research streams

Each stream has a distinct data and methodological challenge while contributing to one broader groundwater-contamination framework.

In progress

Uranium contamination assessment

Approximately 16,756 monitoring locations are combined with about 57 environmental predictors. A separate set of approximately 494 samples supports independent validation. Outputs include contamination probability, risk and transition zones, exposure screening, monitoring adequacy and governance priority.

In progress

Fluoride spatial modelling

Data preparation begins with 72,024 original records and 28,185 spatially unique records; 26,718 contain values and 1,467 are missing. The work compares resampling and resolution choices and investigates deep-learning approaches without presenting preliminary outcomes as final results.

Developing

Arsenic modelling and XAI

The arsenic direction focuses on temporal transferability, robustness and nonlinear interpretation. SHAP and LIME are considered alongside temporal mismatch so model explanations can be judged for stability and decision relevance.

Methodological study

Spatial downscaling

A Punjab pilot uses CHIRPS rainfall from 2013–2022. Original rainfall resolution is approximately 0.05° / 5.6 km; planned outputs include 5 km, 1 km and 500 m, with predictor datasets at 25, 9, 5, 1 and 0.5 km.

Validation questions

Does spatial structure survive resampling? Does the model generalise beyond a random split? Are explanations stable across time and space?
